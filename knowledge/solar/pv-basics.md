# Solar PV Basics

## What a solar PV system is

A solar photovoltaic (PV) system converts sunlight directly into electricity using semiconductor cells, most commonly silicon. No moving parts are involved in the panels themselves — sunlight striking the cells knocks electrons loose and that flow of electrons is the electric current.

## The main components

- **Solar panels (modules):** arrays of PV cells wired together, mounted on a roof or ground frame facing the sun.
- **Inverter:** panels produce direct current (DC); household and grid electricity is alternating current (AC). The inverter converts DC to AC so the power can run appliances or be exported to the grid. See `inverters.md` for the different inverter types.
- **Mounting/racking:** the structural hardware that holds panels to the roof or ground at the correct tilt and secures them against wind loads.
- **Wiring and combiner/disconnect boxes:** carry and safely isolate the DC and AC circuits.
- **Utility meter (for grid-tied systems):** measures electricity flowing to and from the grid — relevant to net metering, see `net-metering-philippines.md`.
- **Battery (optional):** stores energy for use when the sun isn't shining. See `batteries.md`.

## How output varies

A panel's output depends on sunlight intensity, angle/tilt relative to the sun, shading, ambient temperature, and how clean the panel surface is. Shading from trees, neighboring buildings, or rooftop equipment (like water tanks) meaningfully reduces output even if only part of a panel is shaded, because of how panels are wired in series — this is worth asking a customer about early.

## System types, in brief

EnergyWize builds three broad configurations — grid-tied, hybrid, and off-grid — covered in detail in `grid-tied-vs-off-grid-vs-hybrid.md`. Which one fits a property depends on grid reliability, night-time usage, and whether full independence from the utility is the goal.
