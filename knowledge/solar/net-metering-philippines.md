# Net Metering in the Philippines

## The concept

Net metering lets a grid-tied solar customer export surplus electricity — power their panels generate beyond what the property is using at that moment — back to the distribution grid, in exchange for a credit that offsets their electricity bill. It's the mechanism that makes grid-tied solar (without batteries) financially worthwhile: nothing generated goes to waste even when the property isn't using it in real time.

## Legal basis

The Philippine energy regulatory landscape undergoes rapid adjustments, making it vital to cross-reference legal citations directly with the active 2026 directives of the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) and the Department of Energy (DOE) rather than relying on outdated assumptions. Recent shifts are anchored in major frameworks, including ERC Resolution No. 18, Series of 2026, which establishes the implementing rules for entities other than the National Grid Corporation of the Philippines (NGCP) to finance and construct dedicated point-to-point transmission projects. This is complemented by DOE Circular No. DC2026-02-0007, which outlines the structural policy allowing the National Transmission Corporation (TRANSCO) or designated private entities to execute these critical transition lines. Concurrently, the consumer landscape has been redrawn by DOE Circular No. DC2026-08-0017, a directive that significantly simplifies and streamlines the permitting rules for Self-Generating Facility Zero-Export Solar Systems (SGF ZESS) and own-use micro-solar setups. To prevent dynamic guidelines like generation metrics, smart grid integrations, or transmission tariffs from being misstated as permanently settled, legal and compliance professionals must actively monitor live registries through tools like the ERC Resolutions Registry and the DOE Department Circulars Index.

## How it generally works

1. The system has to be grid-tied (or hybrid) — an off-grid system has no grid connection to export to.
2. The property's distribution utility (e.g. the local electric cooperative or franchise holder) has to approve the net metering connection before it's active — this isn't automatic just because a system is installed.
3. A bidirectional meter (or equivalent metering arrangement) tracks power flowing both into and out of the property, so the utility can net the two directions against each other. Credits from that netting apply directly against the customer's consumption on their bill.

## What happens to unused credits

Under ERC Resolution No. 15, Series of 2025, any unused net-metering credits that exceed a billing period's total consumption are subject to indefinite, automatic rollover rather than forfeiture. Unused credits are banked and continuously carried forward to offset future billing periods with no calendar expiration limit. Furthermore, if property ownership changes, these accumulated balances can legally transfer to the new owner upon submission of a conformed letter from the original account holder.

## What EnergyWize actually commits to

Net metering approval is between the customer and their distribution utility — EnergyWize can support the application and technical requirements, but approval timing and terms are the utility's decision, not EnergyWize's. While DOE Circular No. DC2026-04-0011 mandates that distribution utilities process, approve, or reject net metering applications within 10 working days, actual interconnection schedules can still vary depending on local engineering inspections and municipal certificate processing. Team members must not state a specific approval timeline as a definitive promise to a customer.
