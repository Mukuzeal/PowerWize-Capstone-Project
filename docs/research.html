The study

A compliance matter, not an efficiency one

EnergyWize Solutions Inc. is a DOE-accredited Recognized Training Institution. Delivering consistent training, validating payments and issuing authentic certificates is therefore a regulatory obligation, not merely an operational convenience. Those processes ran on email.

Project context

Training material, quizzes and certificates were distributed by email without any integrated or automated system. Because each action, whether submitting a quiz, confirming a payment or issuing a certificate, was performed by hand, staff time went to repetitive work and the process carried a standing risk of information loss, delay and error.

Given that the CEM, CEA and GEMP programmes are DOE-accredited, those errors carry regulatory consequences rather than merely commercial ones.

Rather than rest on general descriptions such as time-consuming or inefficient, the study documented each concern against the evidence that established it, gathered through preliminary interviews and process observation with EnergyWize staff.

Beyond these, the existing setup had no automated tool for Solar PV consultation computation. System sizing, load estimation, panel configuration and cost estimation all depended on manual calculation by staff, limiting both consistency and the volume of clients the company could serve professionally.

Payment verification used no cryptographic safeguards, leaving enrolment-fee records disputable, and certificates carried no tamper-evident validation. As employers and government agencies increasingly expect verifiable digital credentials, the absence of a secure tokenized certificate system is a competitive liability and a compliance risk.

All of these point to one technical hurdle: the lack of a single integrated digital platform that applies AI to CEM, CEA and GEMP training and evaluation, automates Solar PV consultation computation, secures payments through blockchain verification, and delivers tamper-proof tokenized certificates.

Research objectives

General objective. To design, develop, and evaluate PowerWize, an AI-Enhanced Training and Consultation System with Blockchain Payment Verification and Tokenized Certificates, to improve the operational efficiency, data integrity, and service quality of EnergyWize Solutions Inc. in managing its DOE-accredited energy certification training programs, specifically the Certified Energy Manager (CEM), Certified Energy Auditor (CEA), and Government Energy Management Program (GEMP) Training and Workshop, as well as its Solar PV consultation services.

01

To develop an AI-supported learning assistant for the CEM, CEA, and GEMP online training programs by:

  • Analysing trainee performance and learning patterns to generate contextual explanations of complex course materials
  • Providing intelligent quiz hints and personalized feedback based on individual knowledge gaps
  • Guiding trainees through technical concept comprehension and program evaluation
  • Assisting instructors in creating quiz questions aligned with learning objectives
02

To develop an AI-assisted Solar PV consultation computation engine that:

  • Analyzes client-supplied data, including energy load requirements, site location, available roof or land area, and equipment specifications
  • Generates Solar PV system sizing recommendations based on predefined engineering parameters and client-supplied data
  • Reduces the need for manual computation by automating predefined Solar PV sizing and estimation procedures
03

To implement a blockchain-based payment verification module that:

  • Records and validates payment transactions for CEM, CEA, and GEMP enrolment fees and Solar PV consultation fees
  • Produces a blockchain-anchored, tamper-evident payment audit trail
04

To design and deploy a tokenized digital certificate issuance system that:

  • Generates, issues, and securely stores blockchain-anchored certificates for participants who complete CEM, CEA, or GEMP training assessments
  • Enables third-party verification of issued certificates
  • Provides blockchain-based verification mechanisms intended to improve certificate integrity and traceability
05

To build an administrative dashboard that provides EnergyWize Solutions Inc. personnel with tools for:

  • Managing user accounts and access levels
  • Monitoring training completion and progress across all three programs
  • Reviewing Solar PV consultation requests
  • Generating operational reports for data-driven decision-making
06

To evaluate the system's functional performance, usability, and overall quality through functional testing and User Acceptance Testing, focusing on:

  • Functional suitability
  • Performance efficiency
  • Interaction capability
  • Reliability
  • Security
  • Maintainability

These evaluations are guided by selected quality characteristics of the ISO/IEC 25010:2023 software quality standard and the Technology Acceptance Model, administered to administrators, trainers, and CEM/CEA/GEMP training participants. Solar PV consultation clients use the consultation module but are not surveyed as part of UAT.

Methodology

The research design is developmental research with a descriptive-evaluative approach. Agile is the development methodology, not the research design. The study keeps the two apart deliberately, so that a way of building is not mistaken for a way of knowing.

Requirement and analysis

Stakeholder interviews with EnergyWize management and staff. Definition of system scope, user roles and functional requirements. Technology stack selection, sprint planning and product backlog creation.

Design

System architecture, database schema and entity-relationship modelling. Interface wireframing for all modules, blockchain smart-contract schema, AI integration specification, and design review with stakeholders.

Implementation

Sprint-based coding of every module: the AI learning assistant, Solar PV computation engine, blockchain payment verification, tokenized certificate issuance and the administrative dashboard, with biweekly sprint reviews.

Testing

Unit and integration testing, performance testing of the blockchain layer, security testing of payment and certificate modules, validation of PV outputs against engineering benchmarks, and black-box testing by 10 IT professionals.

Development

Iterative refinement from testing findings. Resolution of defects and performance issues, re-development and re-testing of affected modules before reintegration, and sprint backlog updates.

Maintenance

Post-deployment monitoring. Fixes and optimisation from UAT findings, updates to AI models and smart contracts, and preparation of final documentation, user manuals and the deployment package.

Launch

Formal deployment and final user acceptance testing with all 48 respondents. Structured feedback through the UAT questionnaire and the System Usability Scale, final revisions, and system handover.

Who evaluated it

Total enumeration of 48 EnergyWize stakeholders: one super user, one administrator, one secretary, 20 trainers and 25 trainees. Alongside them, 10 IT professionals selected by purposive sampling served as system testers, each with at least two years of professional experience.

How it was measured

Black-box testing on a dichotomous pass or fail scale, and a 25-item questionnaire on a five-point Likert scale drawn from selected ISO/IEC 25010:2023 characteristics and Technology Acceptance Model constructs, with the ten-item System Usability Scale as a supplementary measure.

Where it rated highest

Behavioral Intention to Use recorded the highest criterion mean at 4.03, followed by Attitude Toward Using at 4.00. Respondents reported willingness to adopt the platform and to recommend it to other training providers.

Where it rated lowest

Perceived Usefulness recorded the lowest criterion mean at 3.70, and written feedback identified Solar PV report generation delays, clarity of some forms, and limited visibility of quiz and transaction status updates.

Stated as recorded. A 100% pass rate describes the documented test coverage, not freedom from defects. The figures above are outcomes of the specified scenarios. They do not constitute a load benchmark, an independent security audit, or evidence of sustained adoption after deployment.
